# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One user: James (Somchai), a doctor and recreational marathon runner in Bangkok, 85 kg, VO₂max 55, training four run days a week plus a bike day and two strength sessions after a September 2026 foot injury. He opens the app on his iPhone (installed to the home screen) before and after a session, usually in the evening after work or on a Saturday morning before the long run, and on his Mac when he plans the week or reads the evidence. The job on the phone is: what is today's session, log it, see whether this week is on track and whether the load guards are quiet. The job on the Mac is: read the plan, the race-week protocol, the periodization rationale, and the Strava-derived analysis.

## Product Purpose

A personal, offline-first training system for one season: the Mt. Fuji International Marathon on 13 December 2026 (6-hour cutoff) with the Amazing Thailand Marathon Bangkok Half on 28 November as the pacing test. It turns an evidence-based 14-week plan (Daniels, Fitzgerald 80/20, ACSM, Llanos-Lagos 2024) into dated sessions, records what actually happened (manual or Strava), and applies explainable guards (ACWR, long-run jump, stopped time, cadence decay) so the two load-spike injuries of 2026 do not happen a third time. Success is a finish inside the cutoff and no injury between now and race day.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, coach-like. Three words: **titanium, honest, unhurried**. The visual world is Apple Watch Ultra × Whoop: matte black surfaces, one warm orange, system typography, tabular numbers. It should feel like a serious instrument the athlete trusts at 5 a.m., not a fitness-brand campaign. Numbers are shown with their inputs; advice is never louder than the evidence behind it.

## Anti-references

- Consumer fitness apps that gamify with confetti, streak badges, motivational quotes and gradient hero cards (Strava Year in Sport, Nike Run Club coaching screens).
- Marketing-page patterns inside the tool: editorial mastheads on every screen, numbered section eyebrows, long deks, hero posters that push the day's session below the fold on a phone.
- Spreadsheet-style dashboards that expose every metric at once with tiny uppercase labels.
- Generic "AI dashboard" looks: glass cards, gradient text, side-stripe callouts.

## Design Principles

1. **Today first on the phone.** The session that matters is the one in front of him; everything else is one tap deeper.
2. **One thumb, no chrome.** Installed-app ergonomics: bottom tabs, 44-pt targets, sheets instead of modals, no browser-feeling navigation.
3. **Show the input next to the verdict.** Every guard, projection and pace target displays the numbers and rule it came from.
4. **Data honesty over decoration.** Treadmill distances, stopped time and estimated numbers are labeled as such; nothing is rounded to look better.
5. **Desktop reads, phone acts.** The Mac keeps the editorial depth (rationale, evidence, tables); the phone gets the compact operational view of the same data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target stated; the app is dark-only by design. Body text and labels must clear 4.5:1 on the matte-black surfaces, touch targets ≥44 pt, inputs ≥16 px on iOS (no focus zoom), full `prefers-reduced-motion` support, and nothing conveyed by color alone (state also carried by text or icon). Thai and English copy coexist; layouts must tolerate longer Thai strings.
